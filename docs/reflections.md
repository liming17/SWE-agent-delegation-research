# Research lessons and contribution

## What changed in the reasoning

### From a selected failure rate to the question being estimated

The original A-pass/B-fail conditional summary was easy to mistake for a performance loss. Examining the opposite-direction outcomes made the distinction concrete: nine A-pass/B-fail pairs and seven A-fail/B-pass pairs yield only a two-success net difference out of 90. A conditional description and an average contrast answer different questions.

The methodological lesson is to define the estimand before interpreting a memorable percentage.

### From a successful intervention run to an alternative explanation

A new Developer can succeed without receiving source requirements. The B1 control made that explanation measurable. B0 supplied a separate screen, while the primary comparison remained over all fixed handoffs.

The lesson is to design a control that could challenge the preferred story, rather than only collect examples consistent with it.

### From a benchmark score to a validated measurement

The evaluator audit showed that some scores depended on details not explicitly required by the task text. Original scores were kept, alternative rules were documented and validated, and the entire archived set was rescored.

The lesson is to audit what a test actually measures. Replacing a test does not make the replacement infallible, and a post-collection revision must remain visibly post-collection.

### From an uncertain estimate to a bounded conclusion

The completed source-versus-retry comparison gave a zero point estimate with a wide interval. Reporting that result required withdrawing stronger recovery language while retaining the observations, failures and implementation history.

The lesson is that uncertainty belongs in the conclusion, not only in a limitations footnote.

## Relevance to future research

The project provides experience with **Reliable Autonomous Agents** and the methodology relevant to **Agent Safety Evaluation**: defining observable properties, separating task completion from constraint satisfaction, auditing evaluators, constructing controls, and preserving evidence.

It does not justify calling all software constraints safety properties, establish a runtime assurance method, or identify individual failure mechanisms. Those would require explicit new questions and designs.
